## What the chapter establishes

1. **Ladders imply one direction.** Names Maslow (motivation hierarchy), Dreyfus (novice→expert skill acquisition), and the Gartner Hype Cycle (technology maturity) accurately and fairly, framing them as legitimate tools for genuinely one-directional questions — not strawmen.

2. **Hawkins as cautionary metaphor.** David Hawkins' "Map of Consciousness" is presented explicitly as a *popular metaphor*, NOT a validated scientific instrument. The text states plainly that its measurement and muscle-testing/calibration claims "do not survive scrutiny" and that "the precision is the illusion." It is used as the justification for why this book uses no numeric scales or scores.

3. **Reality is multi-directional.** Two worked contrasts at the same AI-capability level with opposite leverage (brilliant AI researcher with thin judgment vs. junior engineer working only at task level), plus the reverse case (master teacher / CEO with little AI use but high leverage). Concludes that one axis cannot capture it; you need two. "One line can rank people. It takes a plane to locate them."

4. **Instrument, not label.** Distinguishes a *position* (dot) from a *vector* (where you were → are → heading). Emphasizes movement in any direction, not just diagonal "up," including the downward slide (capability up, leverage eroding) that carries the book's through-line.
