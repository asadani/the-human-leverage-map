Yes. **The Map of Consciousness** is a model developed by David R. Hawkins, most notably presented in his book Power vs. Force and expanded in later works like The Eye of the I and Transcending the Levels of Consciousness.

The model attempts to rank different emotional and psychological states on a logarithmic scale from  **1 to 1000** :

|     Level | State         | General Description                         |
| --------: | ------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
|        20 | Shame         | Self-hatred, humiliation                    |
|        30 | Guilt         | Blame, regret                               |
|        50 | Apathy        | Helplessness, despair                       |
|        75 | Grief         | Sadness, loss                               |
|       100 | Fear          | Anxiety, survival                           |
|       125 | Desire        | Craving, attachment                         |
|       150 | Anger         | Frustration, resentment                     |
|       175 | Pride         | Ego, defensiveness                          |
|       200 | Courage       | Turning point toward growth                 |
|       250 | Neutrality    | Flexibility, non-judgment                   |
|       310 | Willingness   | Learning, responsibility                    |
|       350 | Acceptance    | Reality-based thinking                      |
|       400 | Reason        | Logic, science, analysis                    |
|       500 | Love          | Unconditional care                          |
|       540 | Joy           | Inner peace, gratitude                      |
|       600 | Peace         | Transcendence                               |
| 700–1000 | Enlightenment | Rare states attributed to spiritual masters |

The key idea is that **200 ("Courage")** marks a transition:

* Below 200, people are said to operate from "force"—fear, ego, scarcity, and reactivity.
* Above 200, they increasingly operate from "power"—integrity, compassion, reason, and peace.

Hawkins also claimed:

* Consciousness can be "calibrated" using applied kinesiology (muscle testing).
* Books, organizations, people, historical events, and ideas can all be assigned a numerical consciousness level.
* The scale is logarithmic, so moving from 200 to 300 is not a small increase.

### Why it became popular

The model resonates because it:

* Gives people a simple framework for understanding emotional development.
* Aligns loosely with ideas from positive psychology, mindfulness, and many spiritual traditions.
* Is widely discussed in self-help, leadership, and spirituality communities.

### The scientific view

This is where it's important to separate **useful metaphor** from  **validated science** .

The Map of Consciousness is  **not accepted as a scientific model** . The major criticisms are:

* There is **no empirical evidence** supporting the numerical scale.
* The muscle-testing method Hawkins used has repeatedly failed controlled scientific validation.
* The calibration numbers are not independently reproducible.
* Many of the specific claims (for example, assigning exact consciousness values to famous people or books) cannot be objectively verified.

Because of this, psychologists and neuroscientists generally regard it as a  **philosophical or spiritual framework** , not a scientific measurement system.

### Is it useful?

That depends on how it's used.

It can be useful if you treat it as:

* A reflective tool for thinking about emotional states.
* A reminder that fear, anger, and shame often produce poorer decisions than courage, acceptance, or compassion.
* A way to discuss personal growth.

It becomes problematic if it's treated as:

* An objective measurement of people.
* A way to judge others as "higher" or "lower."
* A scientifically validated ranking system.

Viewed as a metaphor for emotional maturity, many people find it thought-provoking. Viewed as a literal measurement of consciousness, it lacks scientific support.
